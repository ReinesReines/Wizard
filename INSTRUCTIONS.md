# Instructions
Wizard is an "easier-to-understand" version of MTG, which in itself is quite complex. Below is a comprehensive guide on how to play Wizard.

# Creatures, spells, and lands
As you may have noticed, creatures have a number next to a red, blue, or green circle. This is their **cost**. As of a recent update, this number is the total mana cost. The colour of the circle represents the colour of mana it requires. For example, a `2` next to a green circle means that it requires a total of `2` mana, with one of them being a green coloured mana.

### Tapping
Tapping basically "activates" a card. When a card is flipped on its side, it is tapped. This means that they cannot be played for the rest of the turn. Some special cards such as slime and bigger slime are vigilant, which means that they are always available and can block multiple times.

### Lands
You generate mana by tapping a land card. These usually cost nothing. This is done by either pressing `tap all` or just by right clicking and clicking on the colour you wish to tap. Some special lands such as `tropical grove` are known as dual lands,
which means they can generate mana of two colours. All you have to do is select them. The number of mana they generate is dependent on their description.

After tapping a land, they are inactive and cannot be tapped for the rest of the turn. At the end of the turn, unless the player has cast `oath`, their mana pool **resets**.

### Creatures
Creatures are your primary way of dealing damage. By clicking on a creature in your hand, you can click on the creature label to transfer your creature into the "active creatures" pile. One important thing to remember is that **creatures cannot target other creatures.** They can only directly
attack the player, or they can block another creature. That is usually how creatures are killed. When playing a creature, they cannot attack on that same turn due to summoning sickness, unless they have `haste`.

When blocking a creature, they deal damage to each other, and combat is resolved from there. However, some statuses may have an effect on combat resolution.

### Statuses
`flying` - When a creature is flying, this means they cannot be blocked by creatures without `flying` or `reach`.
`reach` - Creatures with reach can block creatures with `flying`.
`trample` - Excess damage from combat resolution gets directly transferred to the player instead.
`vigilant` - Creature does not tap, which means that it can perform multiple actions.
`haste` - Creature does not have summoning sickness and can attack the moment they enter.
`unblockable` - More of a joke as only Vergil has this, but this creature cannot be blocked. At all.

### Spells
Spells don't have summoning sickness and can be used to buff creatures and whatnot. To cast spell cards without a target, simply right-click and press "cast". If they require a target, simply click on the card and select a viable target.

# Turn phases
Usually, a turn starts by the priority player tapping their lands to generate mana. This is known as the **opening phase**, or the beginning phase. They can then choose to play creatures, or alternatively, fight.

### Attacking
By right-clicking a card, you can attack with it. The card may be tapped if it does not have vigilant. You cannot end your turn as you have to confirm attacking creatures, which appears as a menu. Clicking cancel cancels your attack and you can redeclare your attackers. Once you have confirmed your selection, priority gets passed to the opposing player.

### Blocking
Creatures with summoning sickness **can still block.** By clicking on the card, you can see a white line on your cursor. Select an attacking card (highlighted in red). Once you have confirmed all blockers, end your turn.

### Combat resolution
Combat is resolved automatically and priority is given back to the original player. You can finish off by casting spells and end your turn.

# Building decks
As I will be updating the game regularly to include cooler cards, you can build your deck around a specific mana colour, or even around a powerful card. The only limits are your imagination (and mine).
